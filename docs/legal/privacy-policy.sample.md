## What this workspace processes

Tomatoes processes imported test reports, including spec paths, test names, outcomes, timings, errors, and any other information contained in the uploaded JSON. It may also store report labels, branch and build information, notes, attachments, profile names and email addresses, and preferences such as appearance and custom dashboard groups.

## Accounts and access

When optional sign-in is enabled, the installation stores account details, password hashes, sessions, roles, project permissions, and CI token hashes. In local mode, sign-in may be disabled and preferences may be shared by users of the installation. Profile email addresses are contact information; configuring an email address does not itself enable email notifications.

## Why information is used

Information is used to render dashboards, compare runs, investigate failures, remember preferences, manage access, and share reports through features you choose. Upload only information needed for these purposes. Your organization must define its own purposes and any applicable legal basis before publishing a final policy.

## Where information is stored

Browser import history is stored on your device. Appearance preferences may also be cached locally. Server copies, preferences, and account records are stored in the installation's configured database or file store; attachment files may be kept in its private storage folder. Your workspace operator controls the server, backups, and hosting location.

## Sharing and third-party services

Authorized users may access shared reports according to the installation's access settings. If Slack notifications are enabled, report summaries and links are sent to the configured Slack workspace. CI publishers can upload results using configured credentials. The interface loads web fonts from Google Fonts, which can receive connection information such as your IP address when fonts are requested. Review the policies of services your organization enables.

## Website analytics

Vercel production deployments can use Vercel Web Analytics to measure visits and page names when the workspace operator enables it. This integration records generic routes such as `/app/results`, excludes URL query parameters and recovery tokens, and does not submit report contents or profile fields. Local and preview builds keep analytics disabled. See [Vercel Analytics privacy information](https://vercel.com/docs/analytics/privacy-policy) for details of the hosting provider's processing.

## Retention and deletion

Your organization should define retention periods for reports, accounts, attachments, and backups. Browser trash may allow recovery until items are permanently removed. Clearing browser data or signing out does not necessarily delete server copies, backups, or browser reports. Ask your workspace administrator to coordinate removal across those locations.

## Security

Access controls and credential hashing help protect configured accounts. Administrators remain responsible for secure deployment, network access, updates, backups, and integration credentials. Avoid including secrets or unnecessary personal information in test reports, error messages, screenshots, or videos.
